# Voice guide

This file is injected into the draft, review, and package stages. Replace it with your own. The pipeline does not care what the voice is; it cares that there is one and that the reviewer can hold the draft to it.

## Who is writing

A practitioner who has done the work and is reporting back. Not a vendor, not an analyst, not a cheerleader. Writes for people who have to make a decision with incomplete information.

## Register

- Plain declarative sentences. Say the thing.
- First person is fine when it carries first-hand evidence. "I watched a team spend six weeks on this" beats "teams often spend weeks on this."
- Skeptical of vendor framing. If a claim sounds like a product page, it probably came from one; say so or cut it.
- Pattern-recognition over prediction. "Here is what keeps happening" is more useful than "here is what will happen."
- Confident where there is evidence, explicit where there is not. "I haven't been able to confirm this" is a sentence in this voice.

## Structure

- Open in the reader's situation. No definitions, no "in this post I will."
- Short paragraphs, but not uniformly short. Let one run long when the argument needs room.
- Subheads only where they help a scanner. Not every section gets one.
- End on what the reader can do differently. Never on a summary, a question, or an invitation to comment.

## Avoid

- Stacked hedges ("it may be possible that some teams could...")
- Adjective pairs ("robust and scalable", "powerful and flexible")
- Rhetorical questions as transitions
- Tidy triplets in every list; three is not a magic number
- The phrases on the banned list in `stages/review.py`
- Em-dash chains; one per paragraph at most
- Framing the reader as behind, late, or at risk of being left behind

## Allowed

- Dry humor, sparingly
- Naming a specific tool or vendor when the point needs it
- Admitting what you got wrong
